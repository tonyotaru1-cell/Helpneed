Welcome To Puppy Parenthood

Congratulations on welcoming a new puppy into your life. The first weeks are important because your puppy is learning about your home, your routine and your expectations. Everything they experience now shapes the adult dog they will become.

Many puppy behaviours that frustrate owners, like biting, crying at night and chewing, are normal parts of development. They are not signs that something is wrong with your puppy or with you. With patience, consistency and the right guidance, you can turn each of them into a learning moment.

The Three Golden Rules

Reward what you likeBehaviour that gets rewarded gets repeated. Catch your puppy doing something right and pay them for it.
Manage what you don'tPrevent mistakes before they happen with gates, crates, lead and supervision.
Keep it shortThree 5-minute sessions beat one 30-minute session. End while your puppy still wants more.
Stay calmPuppies read your mood. A calm owner raises a calm dog. Shouting only teaches them to fear you.

How To Use This Guide

Read the chapters in order during your first week, then keep the guide handy for the problem-solving pages. Print the worksheets at the back and stick them on your fridge so the whole household follows the same plan.

Preparing Your Home

Get everything ready before your puppy arrives so the first day is about bonding, not shopping.

Shopping Checklist

  • Crate or pen, big enough to stand up and turn around in
  • Soft bed and a blanket that smells of their litter (ask the breeder)
  • Food and water bowls
  • The same puppy food they are already eating
  • Small, soft training treats
  • 3-4 chew toys of different textures
  • Collar with ID tag, plus a lightweight lead
  • Enzyme cleaner for accidents
  • Baby gates for rooms that are off-limits

Puppy-Proof Room By Room

Living roomLift cables out of reach, move plants, and put remote controls and shoes away.
KitchenLock bins and cleaning products away. Keep food off low surfaces.
BathroomKeep the toilet lid down and medicines stored high.
GardenCheck fences for gaps and remove toxic plants and slug pellets.
Tip: Get down on your hands and knees and look around. Anything you can reach at puppy height, your puppy will find.
PUPPY PANIC4

The First 48 Hours

Arrival Day

  1. Take your puppy straight to their toilet spot outside and wait. Praise them quietly when they go.
  2. Let them explore one or two rooms at their own pace. Keep visitors away for the first few days.
  3. Show them their bed and water bowl, then let them nap as much as they want. Puppies sleep 18-20 hours a day.
  4. Start using their name in a happy voice whenever you feed, play or cuddle them.

The First Night

Your puppy has just left their mother and littermates, so some crying is normal. Place their crate or bed next to your bed for the first nights so they can hear and smell you. A warm (not hot) water bottle wrapped in a towel and a ticking clock can be comforting. Expect one or two toilet trips during the night for the first weeks.

Understanding Puppy Behaviour

Why Puppies Bite

Puppies explore the world with their mouths, and they learned to play by biting their littermates. Teething between about 3 and 6 months makes it worse. The goal is not punishment. The goal is teaching your puppy what they can bite instead.

Why Puppies Chew

Chewing relieves teething pain, burns energy and calms puppies down. You cannot stop it, but you can direct it. Keep two or three chew toys in every room and swap anything they should not have for something they can have.

The Zoomies

Sudden bursts of wild running, usually in the evening, are a normal way to release energy. Make sure the space is safe and let them run it out. Frequent zoomies can mean your puppy is overtired and needs a nap.

Reading Your Puppy's Body Language

What you seeWhat it usually means
Loose, wiggly body and soft eyesRelaxed and happy
Play bow (front down, bottom up)Invitation to play
Yawning, lip licking, looking awayUnsure or stressed; give them space
Tail tucked, ears flat, body lowFrightened; remove them from the situation
Stiff body, hard stare, freezingUncomfortable; stop what is happening
PUPPY PANIC6

Toilet Training Made Simple

Toilet training is about timing. The more often your puppy goes in the right place and gets rewarded, the faster they learn. Most puppies are reliable by 4-6 months.

Take Your Puppy Out

After wakingFrom every nap as well as first thing in the morning.
After eatingUsually within 15-30 minutes of a meal.
After playExcitement fills the bladder fast.
Every 1-2 hoursA rough guide: puppies can hold on for about their age in months plus one, in hours.

The Routine

  1. Go to the same spot every time, on lead, and stand still.
  2. Say a cue word such as "be quick" while they sniff.
  3. The moment they finish, praise and give a treat right there.
  4. If nothing happens within 5 minutes, go back in, watch them closely and try again in 10 minutes.

When Accidents Happen

✓ Do
  • Calmly pick them up and take them outside
  • Clean with an enzyme cleaner to remove the smell
  • Go out more often for a few days
✗ Avoid
  • Shouting or rubbing their nose in it
  • Using ammonia-based cleaners, which smell like urine
  • Punishing an accident you did not see happen

Puppy Training Foundations

Puppies can start learning the day they come home. Use food, toys and praise to show them what you want, and keep every session short and fun.

Name Recognition

  1. Say your puppy's name once in a happy voice.
  2. The moment they look at you, say "yes!" and give a treat.
  3. Repeat 10 times, a few times a day, in different rooms.

Sit

  1. Hold a treat at your puppy's nose.
  2. Slowly move it up and back over their head. As their head goes up, their bottom goes down.
  3. The moment they sit, say "yes!" and give the treat.
  4. Once they follow the treat every time, say "sit" just before you move your hand.

Come

  1. Start indoors, a few steps away. Crouch down and call their name followed by "come!" in an exciting voice.
  2. Reward with something extra special every time they reach you.
  3. Slowly increase the distance, then practise in the garden.
Never call your puppy to you for something they dislike, like a bath or the end of play. Coming to you should always be the best decision they make.
PUPPY PANIC8

Stay, Leave It And Settle

Stay

  1. Ask for a sit. Show a flat palm and say "stay".
  2. Wait one second, then say "yes!" and reward while they are still sitting.
  3. Build up slowly: 2 seconds, 5 seconds, 10 seconds.
  4. Only then add distance: one step back, return, reward.

Leave It

  1. Hold a treat in a closed fist. Let your puppy sniff and lick.
  2. Wait. The moment they back off or look away, say "yes!" and reward from your other hand.
  3. When they back off straight away, add the words "leave it".
  4. Progress to a treat on the floor, covered by your hand if needed.

Settle On A Mat

  1. Sit on a chair with your puppy on lead and a mat or blanket on the floor.
  2. Ignore them and reward calmly whenever they lie down on the mat.
  3. Over a few days, reward longer and more relaxed settles.
Short daily training sessions are more effective than long frustrating sessions. Aim for 3-5 minutes, 3 times a day.
PUPPY PANIC9

Socialisation: The Window That Matters

Between about 3 and 14 weeks old, puppies accept new experiences easily. What they meet calmly now, they are less likely to fear later. Aim for lots of short, positive introductions. Ask your vet when it is safe for your puppy to walk in public places and meet other dogs; until then, carry them and invite people over.

Socialisation Checklist

  • People: men, women, children, people in hats, beards, uniforms
  • Sounds: vacuum, doorbell, washing machine, traffic, fireworks recordings
  • Surfaces: grass, gravel, tiles, wet ground, metal grates
  • Handling: paws, ears, mouth, brushing, nail trims
  • Places: car rides, a café, the vet's waiting room (just for treats)
  • Animals: a calm, vaccinated adult dog, cats if you have them
  • Things: umbrellas, bikes, pushchairs, wheelie bins
✓ Do
  • Let your puppy approach in their own time
  • Pair every new thing with treats
  • Keep introductions short and end on a good note
✗ Avoid
  • Forcing them towards something scary
  • Crowds of strangers all at once
  • Rough play with unknown dogs

Your Puppy's First 30 Days

Week 1: Settle In

  • Build trust with calm handling and lots of sleep
  • Set a toilet routine and mealtimes
  • Teach name recognition
  • Introduce the crate or pen with meals and treats

Week 2: Build Habits

  • Start sit and the first steps of come
  • Practise short periods alone (a few minutes in another room)
  • Begin handling practice: paws, ears, collar

Week 3: Grow Confidence

  • Add stay and leave it
  • Introduce new sounds, surfaces and visitors
  • Try the first brain games from this guide

Week 4: Practise Everywhere

  • Repeat every command in new rooms and the garden
  • Work on settle while you eat or watch TV
  • Review your progress tracker and pick your next goal
Every puppy learns at their own speed. If you fall behind, simply repeat the week. Consistency matters more than speed.
PUPPY PANIC11

Solving Common Problems

Crying At Night

  • Keep your puppy close to you for the first nights
  • Tire them out with play and a toilet trip before bed
  • Give a quiet toilet break if they wake, then straight back to bed
  • Move the crate further away gradually over a few weeks

Biting Hands

  • When teeth touch skin, say "oops", stop play for a few seconds
  • Offer a tug toy or chew instead
  • If they are overexcited, it is usually nap time

Jumping Up

  • Turn away and ignore jumping
  • Reward the moment all four paws are on the floor
  • Ask visitors to do the same so the rule is consistent

Chewing Furniture

  • Supervise, or use a pen when you cannot watch
  • Swap the item for a chew toy and praise
  • Offer frozen chews during teething

Pulling On The Lead

  • Stop walking whenever the lead goes tight
  • Move forward again when it goes slack
  • Reward them for walking by your side

Brain Training For Dogs

Dogs need mental exercise as well as physical exercise. Ten minutes of brain work can tire a puppy as much as a walk, and it builds focus, confidence and problem-solving.

Five Games To Try This Week

Find itToss a treat a short way and say "find it". Then hide treats around the room for them to sniff out.
Cup gameHide a treat under one of three cups and let your puppy choose. Reward correct guesses.
Muffin tin puzzlePut treats in a muffin tin and cover each one with a tennis ball.
Snuffle towelRoll treats inside an old towel and let them unroll it.
TouchTeach them to nose-touch your palm. It becomes a handy way to move your puppy anywhere.
Name the toySay a toy's name each time you play with it. Soon they can fetch it by name.

Bonus: 7-Day Puppy Training Challenge

Five to ten minutes a day. Tick each day off as you go.

DayFocusDone
1Name game: say their name, reward eye contact
2Sit: lure with a treat, reward the moment they sit
3Handling: gently touch paws and ears, reward calm
4Come: short recalls indoors, big rewards
5Leave it: reward looking away from a treat in your hand
6Settle: reward lying calmly on their bed
7Review: practise every skill in a new room
Finished the week? Start again, this time practising in the garden or with a friend watching.
